Privacy is a luxury. Most of us just never notice it. Privacy draws the line between you and everyone else. People are different because they have boundaries, and they are free because they are different. In _Neon Genesis Evangelion_, the AT Field is a metaphor for the boundary of the self. Every living thing stays its own thing because it has a layer nothing can fully cut through. That layer keeps the world from swallowing you whole, and it is also what makes a real relationship with someone else even possible. This piece is not here to get deep about loneliness. I just really like the AT Field analogy. **Privacy is the AT Field.** The image fits.

Tech moves fast. In theory, we should have more ways to protect privacy than ever. In practice, we are not heading that way. In distributed systems security, there is a classic attack known as an eclipse attack. The attacker isolates a victim node from the real network, just as the moon obscures the sun during an eclipse, so the node only sees what the attacker feeds it. The victim still believes it is connected to the whole network. What it is actually experiencing is a fabricated reality. Something like that is happening in digital life under another name. Social media, search engines, and large language models do two things: they build behavioral profiles from your data, and they decide what you get to see through ranking, recommendation, and filtering. Worse, to keep things cheap and efficient, these systems push content that is simpler, more repetitive, and more stimulating. So people walk into their own Backrooms: spaces that look endless, built out of the same layouts, the same textures, and the same lifeless atmosphere.

So we should leave.

Leave the Backrooms.

Leave the fabricated reality.

Build an AT Field between us and the systems.

There are too many looped voices calling you back. The hits on privacy always show up in the same shapes: "I have nothing to hide." "If you did nothing wrong, you have nothing to fear from censorship." "Data collection is just here to give you better service." They sound like tapes stuck on repeat in the Backrooms. Bit by bit they sand down your sense for privacy, until being watched, predicted, and shaped just feels like normal life.

The road that takes privacy seriously hides another trap. There are always voices trying to package privacy as something you can buy: pay for a service, join a closed system, trust some official fix, like freedom comes back if you just pay the price. Once privacy protection itself gets pulled into consumer logic, it can turn into a new kind of control. People escape one Backroom and walk straight into another.

So welcome to the real world.

This world is one giant chaotic system.

The price of freedom is eternal vigilance.

**_What I am sharing with you is a treasure._**
